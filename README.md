# Hoi, ik ben Sebastiaan 👋

I'm a Dutch PhD student at TU Delft, working on accessible AI for blind and visually impaired readers.

I started out much closer to practice: building software, working with stakeholders, and turning fuzzy requirements into tools that people could actually use. That background still shapes how I approach research. I like structure, I like building, and I tend to ask the annoying but necessary questions: **why are we building this, and who is it actually for?**

Now, as a PhD student, I get to ask those questions in a research context. My work focuses on improving access to non-textual digital content, such as illustrations, maps, diagrams, and figures in digitized books. I investigate how large vision-language models can generate image descriptions that are not only technically capable but also personalized, trustworthy, and meaningful in real reading situations.

In short: I like turning vague ideas into working tools, one line at a time.

## What I'm currently working on

My PhD is still in its early stages, so I spend a lot of time reading papers, shaping research questions, and planning the coming years. The broader direction is clear, though: I want to understand how AI systems can better support blind and visually impaired users in accessing visual content.

That means thinking about things like:

- how people actually want image descriptions to work;
- where large vision-language models succeed, fail, or overclaim;
- how to design systems together with end-users and stakeholders;
- how to make AI tools more adaptive, trustworthy, and useful in practice.

## How I tend to work

I'm a structured and critical builder. I enjoy the messy middle of a project: the stage where there is only a faint idea, a real user need, and a lot of uncertainty. That is usually where the interesting work starts.

I care about technology that solves real problems, not something that looks impressive but doesn't work for the people it's supposed to help. My experience taught me that good tools are rarely built in isolation; they emerge from working with people and real-world problems.

## Things I'm exploring

Besides my PhD work, I am currently interested in:

- 🧠 large language and vision-language models
- 🔒 private and local AI
- 🌍 AI sovereignty
- 🛠️ prototypes that make complex ideas easier to test.
